    Object creator

    Turn your 3D model into a Sims 2 decoration. Use an existing object as its template, then download a ready-to-install package. Everything runs in your browser.

    1. Choose a template

    The template supplies the object's behavior and placement rules. Your model supplies its appearance.

    Select one supported floor or tabletop decoration package, plus any custom mesh or texture packages it needs.

    2. Import and preview your model

    Choose a GLB, or a ZIP containing one glTF scene and its textures.

    Supported models

    Use a static triangle model with UV coordinates and PNG/JPEG textures, up to 2048 pixels per side. Materials can be opaque or use cutout transparency. Bake complex materials and remove animations or rigging before exporting. Model files can be up to 64 MiB.

    Download optimized GLB

    Optional for dense models. Reduces geometry and merges equivalent materials while keeping the original file.

    Optimization settings

    The target is approximate. Higher protection can leave more triangles to limit changes to the shape and texture mapping. Texture images are kept unchanged.

    New models start at the template's original height. Percentage presets use the reference Sim's height.

    Preview display

    Your model preview will appear here.

    Preview shows appearance and scale. Game lighting, interactions and placement still need testing in the game.

    3. Name and create your object

    Your package gets its own identity automatically and can be installed alongside the template object.

    Sim creator experimental

    Fit a humanoid model to an adult Sim. Your work stays in this browser.

    Experimental export: Adult Male Everyday body, with the stock head and other outfits.

    1. Choose model

    Supported models and help

    Choose a neutral A or T pose with arms and legs separated. Use a GLB or a ZIP containing one glTF scene and its local textures, up to 64 MiB. Export neutral geometry without animation, rigging or morph targets. Fitting adjusts body proportions to the game skeleton. The head keeps its shape and has no facial expressions.

    In an A pose the arms point diagonally down. In a T pose they extend sideways. Joint markers belong inside the body, where the limbs bend. Left and right always mean the model's left and right.

    This saved draft uses the previous fitting engine. Its original model and settings are kept.

    2. Fit model

    Each floor square is one game tile.

    Fitting settings changed. Apply fit to update the result.

    Stand upright and face forward

    Match the reference orientation. Feet are placed on the floor automatically.

    Fine alignment

    Place the joint centers

    Drag in Front and Side views. Arrow keys make small adjustments, Shift makes larger ones.

    Check the head boundary

    Gold: rigid head
    Teal: moving body

    Drag the gold line or select a separate part to change its assignment.

    All model parts

    Inspect the fitted model

    Check the neck, shoulders, elbows and knees. Movement is a deformation check, not a game test.
